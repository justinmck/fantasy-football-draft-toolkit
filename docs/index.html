How Important is Drafting in Fantasy Football?

🏈 How Important is Drafting?

I wanted to find out how much the draft actually matters to a fantasy football season — and which players delivered the most value in my 14-team league’s draft last year. I used that analysis to build a live draft board for this season, wired up to real-time recommendations while I’m on the clock.

TL;DR:

  • Draft quality does correlate with how you finish — teams with higher average draft-value tend to place higher in the standings.
  • Value is concentrated in the first ~7 rounds. After that, most picks are roughly replacement-level — you could find similar production on waivers.
  • WRs added the most total value last season, followed by RBs and QBs.
  • Some “reaches” paid off big, and some late-round “steals” were league-winners — the charts below show exactly who.
  • This year’s draft board is live: github.com/justinmck/fantasy-football-draft-toolkit

📈 Correlation Between Draft Value and Final Standings

The first question: does drafting well actually predict finishing well? Yes, moderately — there’s a negative correlation (r = –0.51) between a team’s average draft value and its final standing (lower standing number = better finish), meaning higher average value tends to mean a better finish.

📊 Average Value by Team

Teams with a higher average value per pick clearly tended to finish higher in the standings — though not perfectly. “The Ralph Dudes” finished 7th despite having the 3rd-best draft by this measure, and “Deb’s Debsters” finished 6th (above league average) despite having the second-worst draft. Drafting well helps, but it isn’t the whole story.

📌 Value by Position

Which positions add the most total value to a roster? This sums up value across all starting players at each position. WRs added the most, followed by RBs and QBs.

🎯 Draft Pick vs. Value

Not all draft picks are created equal, and at some point drafting stops mattering much — you could get an equally good player off the waiver wire instead. To find that point, I looked at the relationship between where a player was drafted and the value they delivered. Value turns negative around round 8, pick 2 — past that point, picks are typically not startable and easily replaceable. The first 7 rounds are where the draft actually matters. It rarely makes sense to draft a low-value position like D/ST or K in those early rounds, and even TE value drops off fast after round 7 — if you want a reliable one, get it early.

🧾 Conclusion

The draft is genuinely important to how your season goes — especially in the first 7 rounds, where nearly all of the value is concentrated.

🔑 Key Takeaways:

  • Drafting well matters: higher average draft value correlates with a better final standing.
  • Reaches can backfire, but some bold picks pay off.
  • Late-round steals can be league-winners.
  • Past round 7-8, most of the board is close to replacement level — don’t overthink those picks.

Whether you’re a casual player or a competitive manager, this kind of analysis gives you a real edge on draft day — which is exactly why I built a live version of it. Check out the draft board and full write-up on GitHub.

🔧 Methodology details (for the curious)

Data sources: the full draft order, fantasy points scored, and positional ranks came from ESPN’s private league API. Average draft position (ADP) — treated as “market value” — came from FantasyPros, which blends ADP across multiple fantasy sites into one consensus number.

League format: 14 teams, 2 RB / 2 WR / 1 TE / 1 FLEX (RB/WR/TE) / 1 QB / 1 K / 1 D/ST.

“Value” here is VORP (Value Over Replacement Player): a player’s points minus the points scored by the last startable player at their position, given this league’s exact roster construction (14 teams × starters needed per position, including a share of the FLEX slot for flex-eligible positions) — not an arbitrary or hand-picked cutoff.

This is the same VORP definition used everywhere in the underlying project — the retrospective charts above, the next-season regression model, and the live draft tool’s recommendation engine all share one implementation, so they can’t disagree with each other. The live tool also runs a validated regression (train/test split across seasons, model comparison, and uncertainty bounds on each projection) to estimate next season’s value ahead of the draft.

Full technical write-up — architecture, the regression methodology, validation approach, and known limitations — is in the project README.
